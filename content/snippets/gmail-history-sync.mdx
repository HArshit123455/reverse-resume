## Context

Gmail's push only says "something changed". To find out what, the worker calls `users.history.list` from the mailbox's stored `historyId`. Two traps: history ids are larger than `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER`, and Gmail keeps only about a week of history, answering 404 for anything older. An idle mailbox's cursor would quietly age out and the mailbox would stop syncing.

## What this proves

- Advancing the cursor even on empty pages keeps quiet mailboxes inside Gmail's retention window, and the 404 path turns "cursor too old" into a re-seed instead of a mailbox that is stuck forever.
- Mid-drain cursors are record ids and the cursor is saved per page, after that page's messages are projected, so a crash resumes exactly where it stopped: no gap, no replay.
- Messages are fetched as raw RFC 822 and parsed by the same MIME decoder the rest of the channel uses, and ids stay strings, which avoids a silent 64-bit precision bug.
